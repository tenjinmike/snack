# Product and validation strategy
Draft planning document — 2026-11-07 (Asia/Seoul)

## Product promise
Help small businesses turn Korean/Japanese/English social-media visits into relevant inquiries and bookings through one multilingual page.

## Initial customer hypothesis
Start with independent accommodation and tourism-service businesses. Interview both Korean and Japanese operators before expanding.
Tourism flows are a lead-generation signal, not proof that merchants will pay for SaaS.
Do not describe the total visitor count as the service's addressable SaaS market.
Calculate a bottom-up market only after defining the qualifying merchant count and validated annual price.

## Monetization experiments
| Stream | Experiment | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Subscription | Basic page versus paid multilingual/brand/analytics features | Paid conversion, retention, support cost |
| AI add-on | Included monthly quota with a hard usage ceiling | Cost per active paying merchant, response quality |
| Display ads | Optional free-tier slot away from primary booking buttons | Viewable impressions, net revenue, booking conversion impact |
| Direct sponsorship | Relevant tourism/business partner placement with disclosure | Signed paid pilot and effective revenue per impression |
| Affiliate links | Explicitly disclosed partner offers | Approved program terms and attributable net commission |

Earlier KRW 2,900/9,900 prices remain unvalidated. Do not promise unlimited AI.
Do not assume ad-network approval or ad revenue.
Net ad revenue estimate = monetized impressions / 1,000 × measured net RPM.
Compare ad revenue against changes in inquiry/booking conversion, loading performance, and trust.
For user-generated pages: add content moderation, advertiser controls, privacy/consent review, and network-policy review before ad activation.

## Korea/Japan validation
Planning targets, not current achievements:
- 10 Korean and 10 Japanese merchant interviews; record actual workflow, channel use, willingness to pay.
- Recruit 5 pilots per market before targeting 30 total pilot merchants.
- Test Korean/Japanese copy with native readers; assess terminology and cultural fit.
- Compare Naver/Kakao and LINE/social contact-link preferences in interviews.
- Record completed inquiries/bookings only through explicit first-party events or authorized integrations; outbound clicks alone are not completed bookings.
- Evaluate mobile accessibility, page speed, and merchant activation/retention.

## Grant preparation
BlueIT's known start date is 2024-06-01. Eligibility depends on the final call's reference date, company history, and other criteria.
Treat early-startup programs as a candidate; do not assume eligibility for scale-up programs merely because the calendar year is 2027.
An ordinary LLM API integration is not evidence of deep-tech differentiation.
Prepare: dated interviews, pilot consent, prototype history, localization evaluation, measurable experiments, team execution plan, and itemized eligible spending.
Record external API contributions separately from original classification/recommendation methods.
Government funds are not operating revenue. Do not assume selection, a maximum award, compatible overlapping awards, or reimbursement of pre-award expenses.
The currently located 2027 notice is for program operating institutions, not a confirmed applicant-company funding offer.

## Decision gates
1. Merchant problem and willingness-to-pay validated before expanding features.
2. Authentication and tenant isolation verified before real user onboarding.
3. Measured AI/support cost fits subscription price before paid launch.
4. Ad revenue outweighs merchant-conversion losses before broader ad rollout.
5. Japanese paid pilots and retention justify Japan expansion.
